The trailer sat on remote land in rural Georgia, surrounded by enough darkness that at night the windows stopped behaving like windows.

During the day they showed trees, dirt, chickens, sky.

At night they reflected the room.

You could stand at one and look outward and see only yourself.

There are worse metaphors.

The trailer itself was beautiful. Custom carpentry. Tin work. Somebody had made the small space with care, fitted things together deliberately, imposed order on twenty-five feet of enclosure.

By then the order had failed.

Clothes accumulated. Garbage accumulated. Dishes accumulated. Dog hair colonized the corners. There were no sheets on the bed. Beside it sat an ashtray overflowing with the remains of previous attempts to sit exactly where I was sitting now and understand something.
