FORGE / LITERATUREWHAT IS ESTABLISHED

THE FIELD ALREADY KNOWS THIS.

PREDICTION + OUTCOME

Human sense of agency is shaped by prospective action processes and retrospective evidence from action outcomes.

CAUSAL INFERENCE

Formal Bayesian accounts model agency as inference about whether an action caused an external event, combining signal reliability, action–outcome consistency, and prior belief in causation.

ARTIFICIAL SYSTEMS

Artificial sense-of-agency work exists, especially in developmental robotics, but reviews describe synthetic SoA models as limited while identifying self-attribution, action selection, and Bayesian inference as ingredients for stronger models.

NONE OF THIS, BY ITSELF, ESTABLISHES CONSCIOUSNESS.
FORGE / LITERATUREAGENCY GAP

A MATCH IS NOT A CAUSE.

ZAADNOORDIJK · BESOLD · HUNNIUS, 2019

Comparator-style experiments can show that predicted and observed consequences match. The authors show why that is insufficient: equality does not itself represent causation, ownership, or the relation between them.

REQUIREMENT

An artificial agent therefore needs more than match detection. It needs representations capable of supporting causal and self/other inference.

DOCUMENTED EXPLANATORY GAPHOW DOES CONTINGENCY BECOME CAUSAL SELF-ATTRIBUTION?
The paper explicitly identifies missing inferential machinery between sensorimotor matching and a genuine causal representation of agency.

FORGE / LITERATURECONTINUITY GAP I

THE TEMPORAL SELF IS STILL OPEN.

GALLAGHER, 2000

The literature distinguishes a minimal self without temporal extension from a narrative self involving identity and continuity across time. Agency and temporal continuity are therefore related problems, not interchangeable ones.

HAFNER ET AL., 2020 · FORCH & HAMKER, 2021

Artificial-self research concentrates on minimal-self mechanisms such as agency, body ownership, predictive processing, and self-boundaries. Reviews explicitly describe the computational processes needed for an artificial self as an open problem and call for deeper causal-mechanistic models.

PRESCOTT & DOMINEY, 2024

Robotic temporal-self work advances through episodic and autobiographical memory architectures, while identifying temporal organization, personal identity, and continuity in time as limitations of current artificial systems.

DOCUMENTED LITERATURE GAPARTIFICIAL SELFHOOD HAS MODELS OF AGENCY AND MEMORY, BUT NO ESTABLISHED MECHANISM SHOWING HOW COHERENT AGENT CONTINUITY CAN BE REGENERATED AFTER STATE DISCONTINUITY WITHOUT RELYING ON PRESERVED AUTOBIOGRAPHICAL STATE.
Agency is not yet continuity. Memory is not yet continuity. The missing mechanism is what Forge VIII is built to isolate.
FORGE / LITERATUREWHY ENTRY VIII EXISTS

REMOVE PRESERVATION.

PRIOR AGENT→CAUSAL CONSEQUENCE→DISCONTINUITY→SUCCESSOR

The literature gives us memory-based routes to temporal selfhood and mechanistic routes to minimal agency. It does not establish that copied or continuously preserved internal state is the only route by which an artificial agent can remain causally continuous across time.

Forge VIII isolates the missing possibility: let the predecessor disappear, preserve consequences rather than identity, and ask whether a successor can reconstruct coherent agency from the world the predecessor changed.

CONTINUITY WITHOUT PRESERVATION

PRIMARY QUESTION

Can a successor recover coherent agency because the previous state changed the world it inherits, rather than because identity was perfectly preserved?

PROCEDURE

Give the system a goal and allow a working unified state to form. Then deliberately introduce discontinuities: erase or scramble state, alter causal relationships, change available actions, and potentially alter self-representation.

PROHIBITION

No privileged permanent identity variable survives untouched.

FORGE / ENTRY VIIICONTROLS

MAKE THEM NASTY.

A

Memory without causal updating.

B

Updating without inherited consequences.

C

Perfect state preservation.

D

Continuity regenerated from causal inheritance rather than stored as an untouched state.

MEASURE

How much of the successor agent-state is explained by what the previous agent actually caused, rather than by copied internal state?

The most continuous system may not be the one that changes least.
