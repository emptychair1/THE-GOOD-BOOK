# SOMEONE HOME

Working title for the companion album to *The Good Book*.

## Architecture

- One continuous companion record / playlist per act.
- The book does **not** play, synchronize, crossfade, stop, or otherwise control audio.
- Playback lives externally in Apple Music / Spotify.
- Each act is a human reset point: start that act's playlist at the act opening and let it run continuously.
- Separate act playlists prevent timing drift from accumulating across the whole book.
- The existing reading clock / dynamic reading-speed calculator was built for the abandoned animation system. It may be useful as a design/reference instrument, but it is not runtime audio machinery.
- Do not design sentence-level cues or add JavaScript synchronization. The records must work as records.

## Core sonic rules learned from Record I

### Music is pigment. Silence is substrate.

The book's visual language is **black and white**, not cream. Music supplies richness, temperature, emotional color, and temporal presence. When music is removed, the substrate is exposed.

Continuity is the default. Silence is an event.

- Do not leave accidental gaps between musical ideas.
- Silence must never be a timing patch or transition convenience.
- If the reader is left alone with their thoughts, that absence must be deliberate, targeted, and meaningful.
- Music may become sparse, nearly empty, or quiet without becoming absent.

### Divergence must resolve

Music and prose do not need constant emotional agreement. Productive drift is allowed and can be powerful: the music may feel ahead, behind, or temporarily strange against the page.

**Hard rule:** if the music and prose diverge and never meaningfully reconverge, the track/sequence is not a fit.

Resolution can be emotional, thematic, tonal, rhythmic, lyrical, or retrospective. The reader may briefly wonder whether the music is in the wrong place, but the relationship must eventually reveal itself.

### Entrainment, not synchronization

The reader's pace is not independent of the music. During the first Record I audition, musical motifs and melodic events caused Josh to linger on words/passages and alter reading speed.

Therefore:

- Do not derive record length from static WPM alone.
- Do not force musical events onto exact lines.
- Design for the music and reading to influence one another.
- Timing is observed after the full audition rather than engineered backward.
- Happy exact alignments are welcome but should not become brittle runtime requirements.

### Audition whole acts

Do not ask Josh to audition isolated candidate songs to decide whether they "belong in the book."

Workflow:

1. Research/select candidates against the entire act and established musical grammar.
2. Build a complete sequenced candidate record.
3. Josh starts the act and record together and reads normally from beginning to end.
4. Evaluate the coupled book + record experience first.
5. Diagnose individual tracks only after the full-context audition.
6. Revisions are auditioned again as complete records.

Primary success question: **Did the book and music become one experience?**

## Record I — Act I

### Successful first full-read baseline

Josh's first full Act I audition produced this experienced emotional arc:

> uncanny and slightly out of reach → hauntingly reflective → inspirational → uncanny again → emotionally overwhelming in a beautiful way → woman / talking alone / darkness

The sequence produced moments of apparent musical/textual drift that later resolved beautifully. Preserve that property; do not sand those moments flat merely because they briefly feel uncertain.

On the first audition, Josh reached the **Act II title page exactly as "Mara" finished**. Treat this as observed behavior, not a synchronization requirement.

The music also altered Josh's reading pace. This is important evidence for the entrainment rule above.

### Canonical Record I sequence

1. Dustin O'Halloran — **We Move Lightly**
2. Amigo the Devil — **Cocaine and Abel**
3. Hania Rani — **Glass**
4. Max Cooper — **Order from Chaos**
5. Jack Garratt — **Mara**
6. A Winged Victory for the Sullen — **Steep Hills of Vicodin Tears** — **buffer / coda**

Tracks 1–5 are the designed Act I emotional arc. **Mara is the intended emotional finish.**

Track 6 is deliberate overrun protection for readers who read more slowly than Josh. It preserves Act I's emotional world without introducing a new major statement. A reader who reaches Act II before/during the buffer stops Record I and starts Record II at the Act II opening.

Do not extend Record I indefinitely to accommodate every conceivable reading speed. The buffer handles reasonable variance. Beyond the buffer is jokingly referred to as **Internet Explorer territory** (and, specifically, Gerald territory).

Previously auditioned **Recovery** (Rival Consoles) and **Says** (Nils Frahm) are *not* part of canonical Record I. They were never reached during the successful Act I read and should not remain in the Act I playlist.

## Record I cover / visual grammar

### Palette

- Black and white only.
- Stop using "cream" to describe the light chapters or this project's light visual state.
- No introduced accent color for the album art at this stage.
- Gray may occur as the physical consequence of black and white material mixing, not as a separate graphic palette choice.

### Physical-object concept

The cover should read as a **vinyl sleeve / physical record**, not merely a square digital illustration.

Approved direction:

- matte black sleeve
- restrained white graphic
- no generic AI / neural / cosmic / circuitry imagery
- no literal woman, robot, lighthouse, Tree of Life, DNA helix, or other already-loaded book symbol
- vinyl record pulled partly from the sleeve
- boutique black + opaque white (and optionally clear/translucent) wax treatment; marbling/mixing is preferred over arbitrary decorative splatter
- center label restrained: `SOMEONE HOME` / `I`
- the vinyl's black/white material interaction carries part of the concept

### Approved cover-art concept

The sleeve graphic is an almost imperceptible field of precisely repeated white marks / dots on black. Order is visible first. In one region the repetition subtly disturbs: spacing and relationships change and an emergent pattern appears.

The disturbance must remain subtle enough that it may be missed at first glance, but once noticed it cannot be unseen.

Conceptual question: **when does structure become presence?**

The successful generated version using this dot-field / emergent-disturbance concept is the current Record I cover candidate and should be preserved as the visual baseline. Do not revert to the earlier stringy/random abstract cover treatment.

## Design posture going into Record II

Do not simply imitate Record I's artists or instrumentation. Carry forward the *rules*:

- continuous pigment
- intentional substrate
- productive divergence with earned return
- entrainment over forced synchronization
- complete-act auditioning
- separate playlist reset at the beginning of each act
- music must remain a coherent album even when heard without the book

Record I is the control. Preserve it while experimenting with Record II.
